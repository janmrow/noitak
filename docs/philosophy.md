# Verification-first programming

Noi explores a scenario in which agents produce most implementations and humans have limited time to review them. Under that assumption the cost of establishing what a patch preserves matters more than the cost of writing it. A reviewer of the quota example can examine the conservation equation, its domain, the evidence for that equation and any newly requested authority.

The compiler accepts only operations it can type and interpret. The verifier can contradict the author with a replayable input. The change gate compares the evidence and interface across revisions. A successful proof can coexist with a blocked change, as the maintenance flag example shows. These outcomes answer different review questions.

A human still chooses a suitable domain, decides whether a contract captures the requirement, and authorizes the host's use of results. Small explicit limits let an independent reviewer reproduce exactly what was checked.
